# Primary E6 breaking evidence — bounded conclusions

## 1. The lock is conditional, and the one-pair model actually stops at SO(10)
Bajc–Susic, section 3.1, equations (14)–(20): 27=16+10+1 and 78=45+16+bar16+1. The two SM singlets in a 27 are both SU(5) singlets; 78 has four SU(5) singlets plus the SM-singlet direction of a 24.[1]
For arbitrary copies of 27, bar27 and 78, the cubic superpotential invariants are 27^3, bar27^3, 27*78*bar27 and antisymmetric 78_i*78_j*78_k. On the SM-singlet VEV locus, none contains the SU(5)-24 VEV. The mixed term would require 1*24*1 to contain an SU(5) invariant; the adjoint cubic vanishes on these directions. In particular Tr(78^3)=0 for one commuting adjoint field. The 24 directions therefore occur only in quadratic mass terms.[1]
Consequently F_a=M_ab v_24,b=0 forces v_24=0 **when the adjoint mass matrix is nonsingular**. This is a supersymmetric, renormalizable-superpotential result, not a general prohibition on nonsupersymmetric scalar potentials or freely assigned VEVs. Singular/zero mass choices require a separate flat-direction analysis; do not turn the generic massive result into an unconditional algebraic theorem. Section 3.1 specifically reports that 27+bar27+78 breaks only to SO(10), while extra copies still leave at least SU(5).[1]

## 2. An explicit 351-prime escape exists, but must not be imported into an unspecified theory
Section 4 of [1] constructs an SM-breaking F- and D-flat solution for 27+bar27+351prime+bar351prime. The authors use upper-index 351prime, opposite the commonly used Slansky conjugate naming. Their equation (24): 351prime=1+10+16+54+126+bar144; its SU(5)-24 VEVs are e4 (54) and e5 (bar144), plus conjugates f4,f5.[1]
The superpotential (29), with E=351prime, H=27, is
W=m_E E barE + m_H H barH + lambda1 E^3 + lambda2 barE^3 + lambda3 H^2 barE + lambda4 barH^2 E + lambda5 H^3 + lambda6 barH^3.[1]
The crucial explicitly nonzero cubic invariant (32) on the singlet locus is
I_E^3 = 3(e3 e4^2 + e1 e5^2 - sqrt(2) e2 e4 e5),
and (34) is I_H^2barE = c2^2 f1 + sqrt(2)c1 c2 f2 + c1^2 f3.[1]
Equation (72) gives a nonzero SU(5)-breaking component in the displayed solution:
f5 = m_E / [3 sqrt(2) lambda1^(1/3) lambda2^(2/3)].
Its accompanying D-flat condition is equation (73); this is not a license to choose f5 independently of the remaining vacuum equations.[1]
Breaking alone is not full phenomenological viability: equations (149),(150) show that within the original sector an extra massless doublet implies an extra massless triplet. That paper adds another 27+bar27 pair for DT splitting.[1]
Babu–Bajc–Susic instead add a 78 to the breaking sector. Their equation (1) adds m78*78^2+lambda7*bar27*78*27+lambda8*bar351prime*78*351prime. They exhibit an SM vacuum and successful DT splitting, and fit only the second and third generations, not a complete three-generation precision model.[2]
