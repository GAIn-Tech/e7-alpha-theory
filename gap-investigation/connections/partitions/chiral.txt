# Representations of symmetric groups with non-trivial determinant

###### Abstract.

We give a closed formula for the number of partitions λ\lambda of nn such that the corresponding irreducible representation VλV\_{\lambda} of SnS\_{n} has non-trivial determinant.
We determine how many of these partitions are self-conjugate and how many are hooks.
This is achieved by characterizing the 22-core towers of such partitions.
We also obtain a formula for the number of partitions of nn such that the associated permutation representation of SnS\_{n} has non-trivial determinant.

## 1. Introduction

Let (ρ,V)(\rho,V) be a complex representation of the symmetric group SnS\_{n}.
Let det:G​L𝐂​(V)→𝐂∗\det:GL\_{\mathbf{C}}(V)\to\mathbf{C}^{\*} denote the determinant function.
The composition det∘ρ:Sn→𝐂∗\det\circ\rho:S\_{n}\to\mathbf{C}^{\*}, being a multiplicative character of SnS\_{n}, is either the trivial character or the sign character.
We call (ρ,V)(\rho,V) a *chiral representation* if det∘ρ\det\circ\rho is the sign character of SnS\_{n}.

Recall that the irreducible complex representations of SnS\_{n} are parametrized by partitions of nn.
For brevity, we shall say that λ\lambda is a *chiral partition* if the corresponding representation (ρλ,Vλ)(\rho\_{\lambda},V\_{\lambda}) is chiral11

The use of the field 𝐂\mathbf{C} is not important. Our results hold over any field of characteristic different from two, so long as VλV\_{\lambda} is interpreted as the representation corresponding to λ\lambda in Young’s seminormal form..

## 2. 22-core Tower of a Partition

We briefly recall the definition and basic properties of 22-core towers.
More details can be found in Olsson’s monograph [[5](#bib.bib5)].

For a partition λ\lambda and an integer p>1p>1, let corep​λ\mathrm{core}\_{p}{\lambda} denote the pp-core, and quop​λ\mathrm{quo}\_{p}{\lambda} denote the pp-quotient of λ\lambda.
The partition corep​λ\mathrm{core}\_{p}{\lambda} is what remains of Young diagram of λ\lambda after successively removing the rims of as many pp-hooks as possible.
The pp-quotient quop​λ\mathrm{quo}\_{p}{\lambda} is a pp-tuple (λ0,…,λp−1)(\lambda\_{0},\dotsc,\lambda\_{p-1}) of partitions.
The total number of cells in quop​λ\mathrm{quo}\_{p}{\lambda} is the number of pp-hooks whose rims were removed from λ\lambda to obtain corep​λ\mathrm{core}\_{p}{\lambda}.
Consequently,

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (4) |  | |λ|=|corep​λ|+p⁡(|λ0|+⋯+|λp−1|).|\lambda|=|\mathrm{core}\_{p}{\lambda}|+p(|\lambda\_{0}|+\dotsb+|\lambda\_{p-1}|). |  |

The size of the partition λk\lambda\_{k} in the pp-quotient is the number of nodes in the Young diagram of λ\lambda whose hook-lengths are multiples of pp, and whose hand-nodes have content congruent to kk modulo pp (by definition, the content of the node (i,j)(i,j) is j−ij-i).
The partition λ\lambda can be recovered uniquely from corep​λ\mathrm{core}\_{p}{\lambda} and quop​λ\mathrm{quo}\_{p}{\lambda}.

Given a partition λ\lambda, its 22-core tower is defined as follows: it has rows numbered by integers 0,1,2,…0,1,2,\dotsc.

###### Corollary 7.

A positive integer nn admits a self-conjugate chiral partition if and only if n=3n=3, or n=2k+ϵn=2^{k}+\epsilon for some k≥2k\geq 2 and ϵ∈{0,1}\epsilon\in\{0,1\}.
Moreover, λ\lambda is a self-conjugate chiral partition of 2k+ϵ2^{k}+\epsilon if and only if λ\lambda is self-conjugate and v2​(fλ)=1v\_{2}(f\_{\lambda})=1.
The number of self-conjugate chiral partitions of 2k+ϵ2^{k}+\epsilon is 2k−22^{k-2} for k≥2k\geq 2.

###### Proof.

Let λ′\lambda^{\prime} denote the conjugate of a partition λ\lambda.
Recall that core2​(λ′)=(core2​λ)′\mathrm{core}\_{2}{(\lambda^{\prime})}=(\mathrm{core}\_{2}{\lambda})^{\prime}, and if quo2​λ=(λ0,λ1)\mathrm{quo}\_{2}{\lambda}=(\lambda\_{0},\lambda\_{1}), then quo2​λ′=(λ1′,λ0′)\mathrm{quo}\_{2}{\lambda^{\prime}}=(\lambda\_{1}^{\prime},\lambda\_{0}^{\prime}) (see, for example, [[5](#bib.bib5), Proposition 3.5]).
It follows that the 22-core tower of λ′\lambda^{\prime} is obtained by reflecting the 22-core tower of λ\lambda about the vertical axis, and then replacing each entry with its conjugate.
If λ\lambda is self-conjugate, then its 22-core tower has to be invariant under this operation.
In particular, no row numbered by a positive integer can have weight equal to 11.

If n>3n>3, this can only happen if the case ([6.2](#S2.I1.i2 "item 6.2 ‣ Theorem 6. ‣ 2. 2-core Tower of a Partition ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant")) in Theorem [6](#Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 6. ‣ 2. 2-core Tower of a Partition ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant") is realized with n=2k+ϵn=2^{k}+\epsilon and v=1v=1.

Now suppose λ\lambda is a self-conjugate partition of 2k+ϵ2^{k}+\epsilon and v2​(fλ)=1v\_{2}(f\_{\lambda})=1.
By Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 ([, Proposition 6.4]). ‣ 2. 2-core Tower of a Partition ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant"), wk−1​(λ)=2w\_{k-1}(\lambda)=2, wi​(λ)=0w\_{i}(\lambda)=0 for 0<i<k−10<i<k-1, and w0​(λ)=ϵw\_{0}(\lambda)=\epsilon.
Let xx and yy be the two binary sequences of length k−1k-1 which index the non-empty entries in the 22-core tower of λ\lambda.
The symmetry of the 22-core tower ensures that one begins with 00 and the other with 11.
Thus λ\lambda satisfies the conditions in ([6.2](#S2.I1.i2 "item 6.2 ‣ Theorem 6. ‣ 2. 2-core Tower of a Partition ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant")), and is therefore chiral.

In order to count the number of such partitions, note that each of xx and yy determine each other, and for xx beginning with 00, there are 2k−22^{k-2} choices.
∎

The proof of Theorem [6](#Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 6. ‣ 2. 2-core Tower of a Partition ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant") requires the characterization of chiral partitions in terms of counting a class of Young tableaux, which we take up in the next section.

## 3. Counting Tableaux

In Young’s seminormal form, the representation VλV\_{\lambda} has basis indexed by standard tableaux of shape λ\lambda:

|  |  |  |

The vectors vTv\_{T} are all eigenvectors for the involution ρλ​(s1)\rho\_{\lambda}(s\_{1}).

|  |  |  |  |

## 4. Proof of Theorem [6](#Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 6. ‣ 2. 2-core Tower of a Partition ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant")

The 22-core tower of a partition records how it can be reduced to its core by removing maximal rim-hooks whose sizes are powers of two.
Theorem [6](#Thmtheorem6 "Theorem 6. ‣ 2. 2-core Tower of a Partition ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant") will be proved by analyzing the contributions of the contents of these rim-hooks to C⁡(λ)C(\lambda).
This is achieved in two parts: the first is a reduction to the case where n=ϵ+2kn=\epsilon+2^{k}, with ϵ∈{0,1}\epsilon\in\{0,1\} (Lemma [12](#Thmtheorem12 "Lemma 12. ‣ 4. Proof of Theorem ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant")), and the second is a characterization of chiral partitions of ϵ+2k\epsilon+2^{k} (Lemma [13](#Thmtheorem13 "Lemma 13. ‣ 4. ## 5. The Growth of b⁡(n)b(n) and Sage code

Recall that, if nn is a positive integer with binary expansion ([1](#S1.E1 "In Theorem 1. ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant")),

It has a nice interpretation in terms of Theorem [5](#Thmtheorem5 "Theorem 5 ([, Proposition 6.4]). ‣ 2. 2-core Tower of a Partition ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant"):

## 6. Chiral Hooks

Hooks, namely partitions of the form h⁡(a,b)=(a+1,1b)h(a,b)=(a+1,1^{b}) for a,b≥0a,b\geq 0, form an order ideal in Young’s lattice.
For a partition λ\lambda of nn, let λ−\lambda^{-} denote the set of partitions of n−1n-1 whose Young diagrams are obtained by removing a box from the Young diagram of λ\lambda.
The statistic fλf\_{\lambda} is given by the recursive rule

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | fλ=∑μ∈λ−fμ,f\_{\lambda}=\sum\_{\mu\in\lambda^{-}}f\_{\mu}, |  |

with the initial condition f(1)=1f\_{(1)}=1.
For hooks, this can be rewritten as:

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | fh⁡(a,b)=fh⁡(a−1,b)+fh⁡(a,b−1),f\_{h(a,b)}=f\_{h(a-1,b)}+f\_{h(a,b-1)}, |  |

with the initial condition fh⁡(0,0)=0f\_{h(0,0)}=0.
This is nothing but the ancient rule of the Sanskrit prosodist Pingala for generating the *Meru Prastaara* (now known as Pascal’s triangle; see [[6](#bib.bib6)]).
It is well-known (see Theorem [18](#Thmtheorem18 "Theorem 18. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant")) that the number of odd entries in the nnth row of the Meru Prastaara (i.e., the number of integers 0≤k≤n0\leq k\leq n for which (nk)\binom{n}{k} is odd) is 2ν⁡(n)2^{\nu(n)}.

Chirality depends on the parity of gλg\_{\lambda} which, like fλf\_{\lambda}, satisfies the recursive identity

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | gλ=∑μ∈λ−gμ,g\_{\lambda}=\sum\_{\mu\in\lambda^{-}}g\_{\mu}, |  |

but only for |λ|>2|\lambda|>2, and with initial conditions g(2)=0g\_{(2)}=0 and g(1,1)=1g\_{(1,1)}=1.

|  |  |  |

## 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations

### 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations

For a positive integer

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (19) |  | n=2k0+⋯+2kr, 0≤k0<⋯<kr,n=2^{k\_{0}}+\dotsb+2^{k\_{r}},\,0\leq k\_{0}<\dotsb<k\_{r}, |  |

define bin⁡(n)={k0,…,kr}\bin(n)=\{k\_{0},\ldots,k\_{r}\}, a set of cardinality ν⁡(n)\nu(n).
We say that a partition λ=(λ1,…,λl)\lambda=(\lambda\_{1},\dotsc,\lambda\_{l}) of nn is *neat* when bin⁡(n)\bin(n) is the disjoint union of bin⁡(λ1),…,bin⁡(λl)\bin(\lambda\_{1}),\dotsc,\bin(\lambda\_{l}).
Thus, the number of neat partitions of nn is the Bell number Bν⁡(n)B\_{\nu(n)}.
Given λ\lambda and a part λi\lambda\_{i} of λ\lambda with bin⁡(λi)⊆bin⁡(n)\bin(\lambda\_{i})\subseteq\bin(n), note that λ\lambda is neat if and only if the partition obtained by removing λi\lambda\_{i} from λ\lambda is neat.

###### Theorem 16.

(Characterization) Let λ\lambda be a partition of nn. Then 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] is chiral if and only if one of the following holds:

λ\lambda has exactly two or three odd parts, and the partition obtained by removing these odd parts from λ\lambda is neat.

λ\lambda has exactly one odd part aa, bin⁡(a)⊆bin⁡(n−2)\bin(a)\subseteq\bin(n-2), and the partition obtained by removing aa from λ\lambda is neat.

The proof of Theorem [16](#Thmtheorem16 "Theorem 16. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant") begins with the following lemma:

###### Lemma 17.

For a partition λ=(λ1,…,λl)\lambda=(\lambda\_{1},\dotsc,\lambda\_{l}) of nn, the representation 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] is chiral if and only if

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ∑1≤i<j≤l(n−2λ1,…,λi−1,…,λj−1,…,λl)​ is odd.\sum\_{1\leq i<j\leq l}\binom{n-2}{\lambda\_{1},\dotsc,\lambda\_{i}-1,\dotsc,\lambda\_{j}-1,\dotsc,\lambda\_{l}}\text{ is odd.} |  |

###### Proof.

Consider the action of s1∈Sns\_{1}\in S\_{n} on XλX\_{\lambda}.
Its orbits in XλX\_{\lambda} are of cardinality 11 or 22.
The representation 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] is chiral if and only if the number of orbits of size 22 is odd.
The orbits of size two are formed by elements of XλX\_{\lambda} where 11 and 22 occur in different parts of the partition.
Such an orbit is determined by a set-partition of {3,4,…,n}\{3,4,\dotsc,n\} obtained by removing 11 and 22 from the set-partitions in the orbits.
This gives the formula in the lemma.
∎

The following result is well-known (see for example, [[2](#bib.bib2), Theorem 1]), and tacitly used throughout this section:

###### Theorem 18.

For a partition λ\lambda of nn, the multinomial coefficient

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | (nλ1,…,λl)\binom{n}{\lambda\_{1},\dotsc,\lambda\_{l}} |  |

is odd if and only if λ\lambda is neat.

Remark: This implies that the number of partitions λ\lambda of nn so that the dimension of 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] is odd is equal to Bν⁡(n)B\_{\nu(n)}.

###### Lemma 19.

If the partition λ=(λ1,…,λl)\lambda=(\lambda\_{1},\dotsc,\lambda\_{l}) has either no odd parts or at least four odd parts, then 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] is not chiral.
Consequently, if nn is even and 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] is chiral, then λ\lambda has exactly two odd parts.
If nn is odd and 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] is chiral, then λ\lambda has exactly one or three odd parts.

###### Proof.

By Theorem [18](#Thmtheorem18 "Theorem 18. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant"), each of the summands in Lemma [17](#Thmtheorem17 "Lemma 17. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant") will be even if either of the hypotheses is true, because at least two of the integers in the partitions obtained by reducing two parts of λ\lambda by 11 will be odd.
∎

Here is an ingredient for the case that λ\lambda has exactly three odd parts; its proof is immediate from Theorem [18](#Thmtheorem18 "Theorem 18. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant").

###### Lemma 20.

Let n1+⋯+nm=nn\_{1}+\cdots+n\_{m}=n. If n1n\_{1} is odd and n2,…,nmn\_{2},\ldots,n\_{m} are all even, then

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | (nn1,…,nm)≡(n−1n1−1,n2,…,nm)mod2.\binom{n}{n\_{1},\dotsc,n\_{m}}\equiv\binom{n-1}{n\_{1}-1,n\_{2},\dotsc,n\_{m}}\mod 2. |  |

###### Lemma 21.

Suppose λ\lambda has exactly one odd part aa. Then 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] is chiral if and only if both of the following hold:

bin⁡(a)⊆bin⁡(n−2)\bin(a)\subseteq\bin(n-2).

The partition obtained by removing aa from λ\lambda is neat.

###### Proof.

Write (λ1,…,λm)(\lambda\_{1},\ldots,\lambda\_{m}) for the partition obtained by removing aa from λ\lambda.
Elementary properties of multinomial coefficients give

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ∑i=1m(n−2a−1,λ1,…,λi−1,…,λm)=(n−2a−1)​∑i=1m(n−a−1λ1,…,λi−1,…,λm)=(n−2a−1)​(n−aλ1,…,λm).\begin{split}\sum\_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n-2}{a-1,\lambda\_{1},\ldots,\lambda\_{i}-1,\ldots,\lambda\_{m}}&=\binom{n-2}{a-1}\sum\_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n-a-1}{\lambda\_{1},\ldots,\lambda\_{i}-1,\ldots,\lambda\_{m}}\\ &=\binom{n-2}{a-1}\binom{n-a}{\lambda\_{1},\ldots,\lambda\_{m}}.\end{split} |  |

The result follows, since the other terms from Lemma [17](#Thmtheorem17 "Lemma 17. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant") are necessarily even.
∎

We have all we need to prove the characterization:

###### Proof of Theorem [16](#Thmtheorem16 "Theorem 16. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant").

Lemma [19](#Thmtheorem19 "Lemma 19. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant") breaks the problem into the cases of 1,21,2, or 33 odd parts. The case of one odd part is Lemma [21](#Thmtheorem21 "Lemma 21. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant"). The case of two odd parts is straightforward using Lemma [17](#Thmtheorem17 "Lemma 17. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. ∎

### 7.2. Counting of Chiral Permutation Representations

###### Lemma 22.

Let n≥2n\geq 2 be even. The number of partitions λ\lambda of nn with exactly two odd parts and with 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] chiral, is equal to

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | (Bν⁡(n−2)+2−Bν⁡(n−2)+1+Bν⁡(n−2))/2.(B\_{\nu(n-2)+2}-B\_{\nu(n-2)+1}+B\_{\nu(n-2)})/2. |  |

###### Proof.

We must count the number of partitions λ\lambda of nn which have exactly two odd parts, and so that the partition obtained by removing these odd parts from λ\lambda is neat. Equivalently, the partition of n−2n-2 obtained by subtracting 11 from these odd parts is neat.
Thus we need to count the number of set-partitions of bin⁡(n−2)\bin(n-2) with two marked parts, these two parts being allowed to be empty (adding 11 to these parts will give the desired partition of nn). To do this, add two new elements, say aa and bb, to the set bin⁡(n−2)\bin(n-2).
This set has Bν⁡(n−2)+2B\_{\nu(n-2)+2} set-partitions, but Bν⁡(n−1)+1B\_{\nu(n-1)+1} of these have aa and bb in the same part, and should be excluded from counting.
Except when aa and bb both lie in singleton parts (and there are Bν⁡(n−2)B\_{\nu(n-2)} such partitions), each of these set partitions of bin⁡(n−2)⊔{a,b}\bin(n-2)\sqcup\{a,b\} give rise to two set-partitions of bin⁡(n−2)\bin(n-2) with two marked parts.
So the number of set-partitions of bin⁡(n−2)\bin(n-2) with two marked parts, where the marked parts are not both empty, is given by

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | (Bν⁡(n−2)+2−Bν⁡(n−2)+1−Bν⁡(n−2))/2.(B\_{\nu(n-2)+2}-B\_{\nu(n-2)+1}-B\_{\nu(n-2)})/2. |  |

There remain the set partitions of bin⁡(n−2)\bin(n-2) with two marked parts where both marked parts are empty.
By simply ignoring the marked parts, these are in bijective correspondence with the Bν⁡(n−2)B\_{\nu(n-2)} set-partitions of bin⁡(n−2)\bin(n-2).
The lemma follows.
∎

###### Lemma 23.

Let n≥3n\geq 3 be odd. The number of partitions λ\lambda of nn with exactly three odd parts and with 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] chiral, is equal to

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | 16​(Bν⁡(n−3)+3−3​Bν⁡(n−3)+2+5​Bν⁡(n−3)+1+2​Bν⁡(n−3)).\frac{1}{6}(B\_{\nu(n-3)+3}-3B\_{\nu(n-3)+2}+5B\_{\nu(n-3)+1}+2B\_{\nu(n-3)}). |  |

###### Proof.

This is similar to the previous calculation. Such partitions of nn correspond to neat partitions of n−3n-3 with three distinguished parts. Once again, adding three elements to bin⁡(n−3)\bin(n-3) and making the necessary corrections gives the result.
∎

The part of c⁡(n)c(n) corresponding to partitions with exactly one odd part is surprisingly difficult to compute, although the answer is simple.

###### Theorem 24.

Let n≥1n\geq 1 be odd. The number of partitions λ\lambda of nn with exactly one odd part and with 𝐂⁡[Xλ]\mathbf{C}[X\_{\lambda}] chiral, is equal to

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Bν⁡(n)+k−2+Bν⁡(n)−2​Bν⁡(n)−1,B\_{\nu(n)+k-2}+B\_{\nu(n)}-2B\_{\nu(n)-1}, |  |

###### Proof.

The number of partitions satisfying the conditions of Lemma [21](#Thmtheorem21 "Lemma 21. ‣ 7.1. Characterization of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant") is

|  |  |  |  |

where k=v2​(n−1)k=v\_{2}(n-1), and ν⁡(n)\nu(n) is the number of 11’s in the binary expansion of nn.

The reason is as follows: if v2​(n−1)=kv\_{2}(n-1)=k, then the binary expansions of nn and n−2n-2 are of the form:

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | n=∗∗…∗10…01↑k↓n−2=∗∗…∗01…11\begin{matrix}n=&\*&\*&\dotsc&\*&1&0&\dotsc&0&1\\ &&&&&\uparrow&&&&\\ &&&&&k&&&&\\ &&&&&\downarrow&&&&\\ n-2=&\*&\*&\dotsc&\*&0&1&\dotsc&1&1\\ \end{matrix} |  |

The odd part aa is obtained by choosing bin⁡(a)⊆bin⁡(n−2)\bin(a)\subseteq\bin(n-2).
Suppose that bin⁡(a)\bin(a) has an element 1<i≤k−11<i\leq k-1.
Let ss be the least such ii.
Suppose |bin⁡(a)∩{k+1,k+2,…}|=r|\bin(a)\cap\{k+1,k+2,\dots\}|=r and |bin⁡(a)∩{s+1,…,k−1}|=t|\bin(a)\cap\{s+1,\dots,k-1\}|=t.
The number of ways of choosing these subsets is (ν⁡(n)−2r)×(k−s−1r)\binom{\nu(n)-2}{r}\times\binom{k-s-1}{r}.
Once these are chosen, |bin⁡(n−a)∩{k+1,k+2,…}|=ν⁡(n)−2−r|\bin(n-a)\cap\{k+1,k+2,\dotsc\}|=\nu(n)-2-r.
Also, |bin⁡(n−a)∩{s+1,…,k−1}|=k−s−1−t|\bin(n-a)\cap\{s+1,\dots,k-1\}|=k-s-1-t.
So

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | |bin⁡(n−a)|=[ν⁡(n)−2−r]+[k−s−1−t]+1,|\bin(n-a)|=[\nu(n)-2-r]+[k-s-1-t]+1, |  |

giving rise to the first term.

The second term corresponds to those values of aa for which bin⁡(a)∩{2,…,k−1}=∅\bin(a)\cap\{2,\dots,k-1\}=\emptyset.
Let r=|bin⁡(a)∩{k+1,k+2,…}|r=|\bin(a)\cap\{k+1,k+2,\dotsc\}|.
The number of choices for bin⁡(a)\bin(a) is then (ν⁡(n)−2r)\binom{\nu(n)-2}{r}. This concludes our explanation for the expression ([20](#S7.E20 "In Proof. ‣ 7.2. Counting of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant")).

For a sequence fnf\_{n}, recall from finite difference calculus the jjth differential of fnf\_{n}, a sequence defined by:

|  |  |  |

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | fm+n=∑i=0n(ni)​(Δi​f)m.f\_{m+n}=\sum\_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(\Delta^{i}f)\_{m}. |  |

###### Lemma 25.

For j≥0j\geq 0, we have

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ∑i=0n(ni)​Bn+j−i=(Δj​B)n+1.\sum\_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}B\_{n+j-i}=(\Delta^{j}B)\_{n+1}. |  |

###### Proof.

Start with the explicit expression for the right hand side,

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ∑i=0j(−1)i​(ji)​Bn+1+j−i.\sum\_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\binom{j}{i}B\_{n+1+j-i}. |  |

Using the fundamental recursion for the Bell numbers
in the above formula, we get

|  |  |  |

We now replace the kk sum by ℓ=k+i\ell=k+i and interchange the ℓ\ell and ii sum to get

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | (∑ℓ=0j∑i=0ℓ+∑ℓ=j+1j+n∑i=0j)(−1)i(ji)(n+j−iℓ−i)Bn+j−ℓ.\left(\sum\_{\ell=0}^{j}\sum\_{i=0}^{\ell}+\sum\_{\ell=j+1}^{j+n}\sum\_{i=0}^{j}\right)(-1)^{i}\binom{j}{i}\binom{n+j-i}{\ell-i}B\_{n+j-\ell}. |  |

An application of the well-known Chu-Vandermonde identity,

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ∑i=0min⁡(j,ℓ)(−1)i​(ji)​(n+j−iℓ−i)=(nℓ),\sum\_{i=0}^{\min(j,\ell)}(-1)^{i}\binom{j}{i}\binom{n+j-i}{\ell-i}=\binom{n}{\ell}, |  |

collapses the two sums above and gives the desired result.
∎

###### Lemma 26.

We have

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ∑r=0ν⁡(n)−2∑s=1k−1∑t=0k−s−1(ν⁡(n)−2r)​(k−s−1t)​Bν⁡(n)+k−r−s−t−2=Bν⁡(n)+k−2−Bν⁡(n)−1.\sum\_{r=0}^{\nu(n)-2}\sum\_{s=1}^{k-1}\sum\_{t=0}^{k-s-1}\binom{\nu(n)-2}{r}\binom{k-s-1}{t}B\_{\nu(n)+k-r-s-t-2}\\ =B\_{\nu(n)+k-2}-B\_{\nu(n)-1}. |  |

###### Proof.

Take the rr-sum inside and apply Lemma [25](#Thmtheorem25 "Lemma 25. ‣ Proof. ‣ 7.2. Counting of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant") to obtain

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ∑s=1k−1∑t=0k−s−1(k−s−1t)​(Δk−s−t​B)ν⁡(n)−1=∑s=0k−2∑t=0s(st)​(Δt+1​B)ν⁡(n)−1=∑t=0k−2(Δt+1​B)ν⁡(n)−1​∑s=tk−2(st)=∑t=0k−2(k−1t+1)​(Δt+1​B)ν⁡(n)−1=∑t=1k−1(k−1t)​(Δt​B)ν⁡(n)−1=Bν⁡(n)+k−2−Bν⁡(n)−1.\begin{split}\sum\_{s=1}^{k-1}\sum\_{t=0}^{k-s-1}\binom{k-s-1}{t}(\Delta^{k-s-t}B)\_{\nu(n)-1}&=\sum\_{s=0}^{k-2}\sum\_{t=0}^{s}\binom{s}{t}(\Delta^{t+1}B)\_{\nu(n)-1}\\ &=\sum\_{t=0}^{k-2}(\Delta^{t+1}B)\_{\nu(n)-1}\sum\_{s=t}^{k-2}\binom{s}{t}\\ &=\sum\_{t=0}^{k-2}\binom{k-1}{t+1}(\Delta^{t+1}B)\_{\nu(n)-1}\\ &=\sum\_{t=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{t}(\Delta^{t}B)\_{\nu(n)-1}\\ &=B\_{\nu(n)+k-2}-B\_{\nu(n)-1}.\end{split} |  |

as claimed. (We have used Lemma [25](#Thmtheorem25 "Lemma 25. ‣ Proof. ‣ 7.2. Counting of Chiral Permutation Representations ‣ 7. Chirality for Permutation Representations ‣ Representations of symmetric groupswith non-trivial determinant") again in the last equation.)
∎
