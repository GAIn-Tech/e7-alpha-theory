## Canonical normalization and sign

Damour–Donoghue, Sec. 2.1, printed p. 3 (PDF p. 4), Eqs. (4)-(6), explicitly use a canonically normalized dimensionful scalar phi, with L_phi = +(1/2)(partial phi)^2 in signature (+---), and dimensionless varphi = kappa phi, kappa = sqrt(4 pi G). Their canonical scalar is not their dimensionless varphi.[1]

The task's -(1/2)(partial phi)^2 is the corresponding healthy kinetic convention in signature (-+++); this signature translation is our interpretation, not a claim that their printed sign is negative. If a starting scalar coordinate has a nonunit kinetic factor it must first be canonically normalized; the mere notation phi/F does not establish normalization.

In Sec. 2.2, Eqs. (11)-(12), and Sec. 3, Eqs. (15)-(16) (PDF pp. 6, 8), the rescaled EM potential includes e, and the Lagrangian is -(1-d_e kappa phi) F^2/(4e^2). Thus alpha(phi) = alpha(0)[1+d_e kappa phi] to first order. A positive coefficient multiplying +phi F^2 corresponds to positive d_e, but increasing the inverse-coupling function H gives decreasing alpha.[1]

Conditional algebraic translation, not sourced E7 physics: for alpha=1/(4 pi H), u=phi/F with fixed F, and H=(8/3)f7+Delta, d ln alpha/du = -[(8/3)f7'+Delta']/H. Locally d_e = -[(8/3)f7'+Delta']/(kappa F H). The input coefficient 8/3 and embedding normalization are supplied by the parent task, not established by these sources. Delta' cannot be set to zero on the basis of these sources.

## Scalar dark matter amplitude

Van Tilburg et al., pp. 1-2, Eqs. (1)-(3), supply the canonically normalized scalar-photon Lagrangian, alpha(t) approximately alpha[1+d_e kappa phi_0 cos(m_phi t+delta)], and phi_0 approximately sqrt(2 rho_DM)/m_phi. Page 2 states square-root density scaling and finite coherence time of order 2 pi/(m_phi v^2).[2]

Arvanitaki–Huang–Van Tilburg, Sec. II, pp. 2-3, Eqs. (2)-(3), (6)-(8), explicitly give rho_phi=(1/2)m_phi^2 phi_0^2 and the abundance fraction rho_phi/rho_DM. Sec. VII A, p. 10, states that the harmonic result applies when the quadratic mass term dominates; Sec. II specifies negligible self-interactions and sufficiently weak couplings.[4]

Use phi_0=sqrt(2 rho_phi)/m_phi, rho_phi=eta rho_DM, not total rho_DM unless eta=1. This is the peak amplitude of a real, canonically normalized, nonrelativistic, locally coherent harmonic field, neglecting velocity-squared gradient corrections and significant anharmonic/backreaction effects; it is not the rms amplitude. The monochromatic phase approximation is limited by halo coherence, with omega approximately m_phi in hbar=c=1 units. Do not equate a static clock-drift limit with an oscillatory dark-matter search without a time-domain analysis.[2][4]

## Heavy-threshold matching

Martens, Sec. 1, p. 2, states matching corrections arise from integrating out heavy particles and depend sensitively on heavy mass splittings. Sec. 2.3, p. 11 and footnote 5, describes logarithmic heavy-mass dependence and notes more complicated mass functions can occur at two loops. Equation (26), p. 12, is alpha_i(mu_GUT)=zeta_alpha_i(mu_GUT,alpha(mu_GUT),M_h) alpha(mu_GUT). The adjacent paragraph says the matching coefficient depends on all mass parameters of the integrated-out particles.[3]

Consequently, if a heavy spectrum depends on the scalar, its threshold matching can depend on the scalar as well. The chain rule requires heavy masses and other matching inputs as functions of phi, a renormalization scheme, a matching scale and consistent running. This consequence is a conditional interpretation, not a computed Delta or Delta'. Martens' explicit two-loop result has restrictions listed on p. 13 (including one GUT-scale vev, no heavy fermions and restricted heavy representations/mass patterns); it is not a general E7 two-loop threshold formula.[3]

## Optical sensitivity

Arvanitaki et al., Sec. III, p. 3, Eqs. (9)-(11), write f_A proportional to (mu_A/mu_b)^zeta_A alpha^(xi_A+2), with zeta_A=0 for optical transitions and 1 for hyperfine transitions. For two optical transitions, the shared Rydberg scaling cancels and delta ln(f_A/f_B) approximately (xi_A-xi_B) d_e kappa phi(t). Their Sec. IV B, p. 7, discusses the optical-optical comparison; Table I on p. 5 tabulates sensitivities. Use the difference of coefficients for a specified ratio ordering, not an individual optical sensitivity alone.[4]

Van Tilburg et al. provide an actual dysprosium spectroscopy search, not a pair of conventional optical clock transitions: the near-degenerate transition itself is radio frequency. Page 2 gives opposite signs for the two isotope responses and compares their absolute alpha sensitivities with optical-frequency measurements. It should not be relabeled as an optical-optical clock experiment.[2]
